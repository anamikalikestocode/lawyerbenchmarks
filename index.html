how legal ai gets graded and my many thoughts on it

Overlap rate. How many of two attorneys' edits to the same contract were the same.
Win rate. How often the stronger answer, the AI's answer or the professor's answer was picked.
Score. How much of a lawyer-written rubric the work met. Vals' Legal Research Bench, Mercor and Harvey's agent tasks are all or nothing. The others are partial credit.
Agreement rate. How often two judges gave the same verdict. A coin flip is 50%.
Benchmark agreement rate. How often two benchmarks rank the same two AI models the same way.

"Attorney scores" is what an attorney scored on the same test. "Attorneys agree" is how often two attorneys gave the same verdict on the same question. * My own computation from published data. Where it needed AI judges, I used Claude models only, so the 88% is inflated and the 28% and 53% are untested on Crosby’s and ContractBench’s own judges. † A legal AI product: Harvey's, or Counsel Stack's for the 78%. ? Collected by the source and not published.

Redlining. Scores rise as rubric goes from fixed points to an agreed standard. Crosby (attorney 28%, AI 30%): each rubric is one attorney’s exact edits, so a different good fix fails. ContractBench (attorney 53%, AI 63%): most points come from the client playbook, any fix that fits passes. Vals (attorneys 80%, AI 59%): law firms agreed to a rubric first. Its AI trails, though, as its early 2025. Agent tasks. Mercor's 77% and Harvey's 27% are both all or nothing. Harvey's rubrics are far longer, so meeting every point is far harder. Agreement. Vals compares each judge with a majority of three attorneys. Counted that way, JudgmentBench's inter-attorney 55% is 66% and its attorney-AI judge 62% is 78%.

Sources: Vals (three studies, 2025 to 2026), ContractBench (Ivo and micro1, 2026), Mercor (2026 leaderboard, 2025 paper), Harvey (2025 contract study, 2026 Legal Agent Benchmark).
